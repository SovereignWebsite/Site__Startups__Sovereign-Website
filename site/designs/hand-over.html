1The three keys

The filesPlain files: no server code, no database, no cookies unless you add them.
The historyA repository in your name with every change since the first.
The addressYour domain, in your own name, when you have one.

2How you got here

  1. The interviewAn AI interview about five things, ending in a one-page brief. This step is “a design that is still being built”.
  2. The materialThe owner supplies what they have: a zip, an email, links or files.
  3. The team buildsThe company says of its team: “Each has a written job description and signs its work with its own published key, under the behaviour policy every site publishes at /policy/.”
  4. Five designsThe owner picks one design; the others are kept.
  5. The hand-overThe site is handed over in a repository the owner controls.
  6. The first changeThe milestone is the owner's first change, made by talking to their own AI.

Sources: 7, 18

3The first owner to take the keys

On 20 November 2026 Kate Curtis-Evans became the first owner to edit her own site herself, through GitHub: the hand-over working end to end.

Sources: 15

4What it is

A team of AI agents builds a website, shows five designs and hands it over. The files and the history are the owner's, and so is the address if they bring their own domain. They can move it anywhere.

It comes in two forms: an open pattern that anyone can use for free, the way anyone can install WordPress, and a hosted version that is being prepared.

“Sovereign” here means owning the site and being able to move it. The sites say plainly that this “is not a legal or certification claim”.

Status, as the company states it: “Launch offer: £50, and €50 on European sites, paid once.” “Decided by Dinis Cruz, 23 November 2026.” And on the same page: “Nothing is sold yet. The hosted service under this name does not exist yet, and you cannot buy anything on this site today.”

Sources: 1, 2, 3, 4, 16, 17
